About This Service

What Is Deep Tissue Massage?

Deep tissue massage is a practical label for focused massage that often uses slower strokes, sustained contact, and moderate-to-firm pressure. It is not a promise that the therapist will forcefully reach a particular anatomical layer, and it should not be defined by how much discomfort you can tolerate.

Ricky may work on muscles and surrounding soft tissues related to your goal, but pressure and technique are adjusted throughout the appointment. Massage may provide short-term relief for some kinds of muscle tension or pain; research does not support claims that massage physically “breaks down” adhesions or permanently corrects posture.

Your session begins with an assessment and consent discussion. You can ask for lighter pressure, change the focus area, decline a technique, or stop treatment at any time.

Reasons Clients May Book

  • Chronic neck and shoulder tension
  • Back stiffness or recurring muscle tension
  • Muscle fatigue associated with desk work
  • Muscle knots and trigger points
  • Neck tension that sometimes accompanies headaches
  • Muscle soreness associated with repeated tasks
  • Hip or leg tightness after activity
  • A focused recovery session between workouts

What to Expect — Pressure and Communication

Deep tissue massage is collaborative. Before your session, Ricky will ask about your specific areas of tension, the history of any recurring pain, and your pressure preferences. This shapes the entire treatment.

Pressure does not need to hurt to be useful. Strong, sharp, burning, electric, or escalating pain is a reason to speak up immediately. Ricky can reduce pressure, change technique, move to another area, or stop.

Temporary tenderness can happen after firmer work, but soreness is not proof that a treatment “worked.” Gentle movement may feel comfortable afterward. Seek advice if pain is severe, worsening, or accompanied by unusual bruising, numbness, weakness, swelling, or other concerning symptoms.

Realistic Goals

What Deep Tissue Massage May Help With

  • Short-term relief of muscle tension or soreness for some clients
  • A temporary improvement in comfortable movement
  • Relaxation and a reduced sense of physical stress
  • Focused attention to areas loaded by work, training, or daily activity
  • A clearer understanding of which movements and pressures feel helpful
  • A treatment plan that can be adjusted based on your response
